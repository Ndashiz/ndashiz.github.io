Chapter 06

Project management & Project Office

Project Office / PMO (Project Management Office)

The team that standardises how projects are run: framing, governance, portfolio tracking, reporting, risk management, methods and tools. Three possible levels: supportive (templates and reporting), controlling (imposes standards) and directive (runs the projects itself). The ad describes a hybrid role: you manage initiatives yourself while improving the practices.

Project, product, programme, portfolio

Project: temporary, with a scope, a budget and an end date. Product: ongoing, it lives as long as it creates value. Programme: a set of related projects working towards one objective (for example the Corporate AI Platform). Portfolio: all the initiatives, arbitrated according to strategy and budget.

Project Manager, Product Owner, Product Manager

The PO maximises the value of a team's backlog. The Product Manager owns a product's vision and strategy. The Project Manager guarantees the delivery of a result within a frame (scope, time, budget, quality) and coordinates the stakeholders. You have been a PO and done part of the Product Manager's job; here you add the cross-functional delivery hat.

Iron triangle

Scope, time, cost, with quality in the middle. If one moves, another has to move. In AI a fourth variable is often added: model performance, which is not guaranteed in advance.

Business case

The document that justifies an investment: problem, solution, costs (build and run), quantified benefits (time saved, calls avoided, revenue), risks, return on investment. For AI, also think about inference cost (price per request) and the cost of human supervision.

Roadmap

A view over time of what will be delivered and why. A good roadmap is organised by outcomes rather than by features, with a Now / Next / Later horizon and visible dependencies (data, platform, legal).

Prioritisation

Value / effort (2x2 matrix), RICE (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort), SAFe's WSJF (cost of delay divided by job size), MoSCoW (Must, Should, Could, Won't). For AI, add data feasibility and the level of regulatory risk.

RAID log

Register of Risks, Assumptions, Issues (problems that have materialised) and Dependencies. A risk may happen; an issue is already there.

RACI

A matrix that says, for each task, who is Responsible (does it), Accountable (decides and answers for it, one person only), Consulted (gives an opinion) and Informed (kept up to date).

Governance

Who decides what and when: steering committee (steerco), portfolio reviews, stage gates (idea, PoC, pilot, production). In a bank, the control functions (Risk, Compliance, DPO, Security) must be brought in early, not at the last gate.

Agile at scale

Scrum (sprints, PO, Scrum Master), Kanban (continuous flow), SAFe (quarterly PI Planning, release trains). You have PI experience: that is a strength, because a Project Office has to synchronise several teams.

Change management

Guiding people towards a new way of working. ADKAR model: Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement. Crucial for the "workforce transformation" programme: an AI tool nobody uses creates no value.

Chapter 07

The Project Manager's roles and responsibilities

The Project Manager is accountable for delivering an agreed result, within an agreed frame (scope, time, budget, quality). They get there by aligning people who do not report to them. They do not do the technical work: they create the conditions for it to get done, and they answer for it when it goes off track.

Responsibilities across the lifecycle

The PMBOK framework (PMI) splits a project into five process groups. This is the skeleton to keep in mind if you are asked "what does a project manager do?".

PhaseWhat the PM doesTypical deliverables
1. InitiationClarify the need and the objective with the sponsor. Identify the stakeholders. Define what success is, with numbers. Check that the project deserves to exist.Project charter, business case, stakeholder map
2. PlanningBreak down the scope (WBS or epics), estimate with the teams, set milestones and budget, secure resources, identify risks, organise governance and communication.Project plan, schedule and milestones, budget, RACI, RAID log, communication plan
3. ExecutionCoordinate teams and vendors, run the rituals, remove blockers, take or drive decisions, bring in Risk and Compliance, prepare the change for the users.Meeting minutes, decision log, intermediate deliverables
4. Monitoring and controlCompare actuals to plan (progress, costs, quality), manage scope change requests, escalate in time, report to the steering committee.Status reports, dashboard, change requests, escalations
5. ClosingGet the result accepted, hand over to run, measure the benefits, capture the lessons, release the resources.Acceptance sign-off, handover file, lessons learned, benefits review

What changes for an AI PM in a Project Office

  • Managing uncertainty. Nobody knows in advance whether the model will be good enough. The PM sets success criteria before the PoC, caps its duration and plans explicit go/no-go points.
  • Data is often the critical path. Access, quality, legal basis: the PM deals with them at framing, not in sprint 4.
  • Bring in the control functions early. AI Act category, GDPR impact assessment, DORA for every vendor, model risk. At the end of the project it is too late.
  • Think about the run cost. Inference cost, monitoring, human supervision, retraining. They go into the business case.
  • The project does not stop at go-live. The PM organises the measurement of benefits (containment, calls avoided, time saved) and the handover to a run owner.
  • Adoption is part of delivery. Training, champions in the teams, communication, especially for the "workforce transformation".
  • The Project Office hat. On top of your own initiatives: standards and templates (including AI feature design), portfolio reporting, the intake and prioritisation process for requests, knowledge sharing within the AI community.

Chapter 08

AI concepts to master

AI, machine learning, generative AI

"Classic" machine learning: a model learns to predict or classify from data (credit scoring, fraud detection, churn). Generative AI: a model produces content (text, image, code). At Belfius both coexist: fraud and AML on one side, Hey Belfius on the other.

LLM (Large Language Model)

A model trained on huge amounts of text that predicts the next word. Examples: Mistral, GPT, Claude, Llama. It only "knows" what it saw during training, hence the need to feed it the bank's information.

RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

First retrieve the relevant documents from a knowledge base (Hey Belfius's "knowledge hub"), then give them to the LLM so it answers based on them. Benefits: up-to-date answers, traceable sources, fewer inventions.

Hallucination

A wrong answer stated with confidence. Countermeasures: RAG and grounding in sources, guardrails, refusing to answer outside the scope, systematic evaluation, escalation to a human.

AI agent

An LLM that does not just answer but acts: it calls tools (banking APIs, changing a card limit, blocking a card). More powerful, therefore riskier: it needs limited permissions, confirmations and audit logs.

Copilot

An assistant that helps a human without replacing them (Hey Belfius for the customer, internal assistants for advisers). Internal copilots are at the heart of the "workforce transformation".

Prompt engineering and evaluation

The prompt is the instruction given to the model. Evaluation (evals) means testing the system on a reference set of questions before and after every change: accuracy, tone, compliance, appropriate refusals. It is the AI equivalent of regression testing.

Corporate AI Platform

A shared foundation (model access, security, data, monitoring, cost management) that every team reuses instead of each rebuilding its own stack. It prevents the proliferation of isolated projects and makes compliance easier.

MLOps / LLMOps

Practices for putting and keeping models in production: versioning, deployment, monitoring of performance and drift, retraining, cost tracking. Without MLOps, PoCs stay PoCs.

Big Data and platforms

Processing data volumes too large for a classic tool. Tools publicly cited at Belfius: Azure Machine Learning, Azure Synapse Analytics, Azure Databricks. Notions to know: data lake, pipeline (ETL/ELT), feature store, data quality, data owner.

Cloud migration

Moving applications and data from in-house datacentres to a cloud (here Azure). It is a precondition for AI: that is where the models and the computing power are available. In banking it is framed by DORA and by the EBA's outsourcing requirements.

Lifecycle of an AI use case

Idea→Framing & business case→Data feasibility→PoC→Pilot→MVP in production→Industrialisation & run→Value measurement
"Pilot purgatory"

The classic trap: many successful PoCs, few in production, for lack of a platform, clean data, risk validation or a sponsor for the run. A Project Office exists precisely to move initiatives from the PoC stage to the value stage.

KPIs of an AI feature

  • Containment rate: share of requests resolved without a human. Hey Belfius: 90.7%.
  • Adoption: active users, conversations per month, return rate.
  • Quality: evaluated accuracy, satisfaction (CSAT, thumbs up/down), escalation rate.
  • Business impact: calls avoided in branches and at the call centre, time saved by employees, conversion.
  • Cost: cost per interaction (inference plus supervision).
  • Risk: incidents, complaints, non-compliant answers.
Chapter 09

Regulatory framework

AI Act (the European AI regulation)

In force since 1 August 2024, applied in stages. It classifies systems by risk level: unacceptable (banned, since February 2025), high risk, limited risk (transparency obligation: telling the user they are talking to an AI, which applies to Hey Belfius) and minimal. General-purpose AI models have had obligations since August 2025.

In banking and insurance, the high-risk uses are: creditworthiness assessment and credit scoring of natural persons, and risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance. Obligations: risk management, data quality, documentation, human oversight, robustness. The timeline for high-risk obligations was the subject of a proposed postponement (the "Digital Omnibus", late 2025). Check the status before the interview if you want to quote a date.

DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)

Applicable since 17 January 2025. Digital operational resilience: ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing and, above all, third-party provider management (cloud, LLM providers such as Mistral). Every new AI vendor goes through this filter.

GDPR

Personal data: legal basis, minimisation, right of access and objection, impact assessment (DPIA) for risky processing, and Article 22 on fully automated decisions. The DPO must be brought in from the framing stage.

Model risk management

Managing model risk: model inventory, independent validation, monitoring. Expected by the ECB for significant banks (Belfius has been supervised directly by the ECB since 2014).

Responsible AI

Principles applied day to day: transparency, explainability, fairness (no discriminatory bias), human oversight, security, data protection. At Belfius the message is "human, empathetic AI". The Lab has a CORM officer to turn it into a concrete practice.

Your assetYou come from onboarding, MiFID and AML/KYC. You already know how to work with Compliance and Risk rather than suffer them. Say it: that is rare among AI profiles.
